Roblox Immersive Brand Worlds

10 years of world, level, and game design across shipped Roblox experiences with 7.5B+ plays

Overview

I have been building on Roblox for 10 years. Before this portfolio became mostly analytics and machine learning, I was designing the worlds, levels, player flow, and progression spaces behind shipped games and branded events. Across the experiences I contributed to, those worlds have generated 7.5B+ plays.

I worked in world design, level design, game design, progression, environment production, build leadership, and Roblox DevRel QA. I designed environments, vehicles, terrain, city layouts, lobbies, expansion areas, and branded worlds with attention to the player journey: where people spawn, what they notice, how they move, where they gather, and what keeps the experience engaging.

A lot of this happened when Roblox brand activations were still early. I worked on projects connected to Warner Music, Sony Entertainment, DC’s Black Adam, Bakugan, L’Oreal, JDRF, and other large IP campaigns. I was also personally commended by Roblox CEO David Baszucki for my work during the early interactive concert era on Roblox, including the Ava Max launch-party period.

The simple version: I learned how to build digital worlds that millions of people could enter, understand, explore, return to, and remember.

How I Thought About The Work

Roblox worlds punish weak design fast. Players do not stop to read a manual. They spawn in, look around, and decide almost immediately whether the world makes sense and gives them a reason to stay.

That meant the environment had to do a lot of work:

  • The first view had to tell players where they were and what was worth doing next.
  • Routes, landmarks, reveal moments, and world hierarchy had to guide movement without overexplaining.
  • Branded areas had to feel big without becoming confusing or losing the core game loop.
  • Progression, social spaces, and repeatable moments had to give people reasons to keep playing.
  • Worlds had to run on lower-end devices while supporting crowds, screenshots, and timed live moments.
  • Client ideas had to become real interactive experiences that players could use immediately.

That is the part I am proud of. I was designing the shape of the experience: the level flow, the progression, the energy, and the reasons players came back.
